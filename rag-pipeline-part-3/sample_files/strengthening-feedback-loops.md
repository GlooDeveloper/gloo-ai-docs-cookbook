# Strengthening Feedback Loops

Healthy organizations learn faster than they fail. The mechanism behind that
speed is a short, honest feedback loop: notice what's happening, make sense of
it, and adjust before small problems compound.

## Shorten the Distance From Signal to Action

The longer it takes for a frontline observation to reach someone who can act,
the more expensive every problem becomes. Flatten that path. The person who
sees the issue should be one short conversation away from the person who can
fix it.

## Make It Safe to Report Bad News

People hide problems when reporting them is punished. Teams that surface issues
early are not failing more — they're failing visibly, which is the only kind of
failure you can fix. Reward the messenger.

## Close the Loop Out Loud

When feedback leads to a change, say so publicly. People stop giving input when
it disappears into silence. Visibly acting on feedback is what keeps the loop
alive for the next problem.

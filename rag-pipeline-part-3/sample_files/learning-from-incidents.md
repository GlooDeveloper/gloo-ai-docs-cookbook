# Learning From Incidents

Every organization has incidents — a missed deadline, a botched event, a
service that went down. What separates resilient teams is not avoiding
incidents but mining them for durable lessons.

## Blame the System, Not the Person

When something breaks, the useful question is "what allowed this to happen?",
not "who messed up?". People act rationally inside the systems they're given.
Fix the system and you prevent a whole class of future incidents; blame one
person and you only teach everyone to hide the next one.

## Write It Down While It's Fresh

Memory fades and softens. Capture the timeline, the contributing factors, and
the decisions made under pressure within a day or two, before the story gets
tidied into something less true.

## Turn Findings Into Defaults

A lesson that lives only in a document changes nothing. The real output of a
review is a changed default: a new checklist item, a guardrail, a removed
sharp edge. Resilience is the accumulation of those small, permanent changes.

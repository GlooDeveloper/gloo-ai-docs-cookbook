# Measuring Community Impact

If you can't describe the change you're making, you can't improve it or
sustain support for it. Measurement turns good intentions into accountable
results.

## Count Outcomes, Not Just Activity

It's easy to report how many meals were served or hours were logged. Those are
activities. Impact is the change those activities produce: families who stayed
housed, students who graduated, neighbors who stopped feeling alone. Track the
outcome, even when it's harder to count.

## Ask the People You Serve

The most honest measure of impact comes from the people experiencing it. Short,
regular conversations reveal what surveys miss. Ask what changed, what helped,
and what fell short — then let the answers reshape the work.

## Share the Numbers Honestly

Report setbacks alongside wins. Communities and funders trust organizations
that name what isn't working. Honest measurement, shared openly, is what earns
the long-term partnerships that real change requires.
